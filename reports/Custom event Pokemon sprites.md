# Run event Pokémon as returning looks

Dexora's first event should not be a new species, a new tier or a dex goal. It should be one evolution line wearing a custom accessory, shipped as appended "look" entries that never leave the save layout. The window is about two weeks. Delivery is one guaranteed claim plus a wild chase on one themed map, the look carries through evolution and can be traded, and the event returns every year. Comparable games have already tried every option in this space. DelugeRPG hands out an untradeable event Pokémon for completing a task. PokéFarm Q separates dex-counting Variants from look-only Custom Sprites. Pokémon Revolution Online (PRO) puts dozens of accessorised "counterparts" into its wild tables at about 1/550 each year. Pokémon GO made costumes mainstream and then wore them out. Players resent four things: missing an event because nobody announced it, costumes that cannot evolve, costumes appearing so often they lose their novelty, and long dead gaps between events. Players value annual rituals, re-runs, and event Pokémon that keep their value. Usable official event art in Dexora's style has run out. PokeAPI's cosplay and cap Pikachu and Spiky-eared Pichu are already in `forms.js`, and Pokémon GO's 218 costume icons are 3D renders marked "educational use only". So the art has to be made. The cheapest route that reads as official is to edit the real sprite: a palette swap, or an accessory drawn once at native scale and composited by a Pillow script. AI output is used only to inpaint an accessory onto the real sprite, never to draw a whole Pokémon, and then has to be cleaned back onto the sprite's own palette. None of this licenses Nintendo's designs. Staying non-commercial and crediting artists is the community norm, not a permission.

## Four delivery models, and what players do with each

Every game studied uses some mix of four models. The first is a **guaranteed claim, one per player**. DelugeRPG gives an event Pokémon "during weekends, holidays/festivals, and special occasions" to anyone who completes a task such as catching a given type or beating set trainers ([DelugeRPG Forums](https://www.delugerpg.net/viewtopic.php?t=30539)). PokéFarm's Halloween 2024 ended with every user receiving one Custom Sprite Gengar ([PokéFarm Q Wiki](https://pokefarm.wiki/Events/Hallowe'en)). The second is a **low-rate wild form**. PRO's 2018–19 Valentine's and Easter forms appeared in the wild at **1/550**. Its 2016 Christmas forms were about 1/750. Valentine's 2024 gave **96 species** a counterpart ([PRO Wiki: Counterparts](https://wiki.pokemonrevolution.net/index.php?title=Pok%C3%A9mon_Counterparts); [PRO Wiki: Official events](https://wiki.pokemonrevolution.net/index.php?title=Official_events)). Pokémon Vortex spawned Christmas Caterpie on grass maps at night until 27 December 2014 ([Vortex Wiki](https://wiki.pokemon-vortex.com/wiki/Events)). The third is an **event-currency shop**, such as PokéFarm's Valentine's 2026 Sweet Heart Stand, which traded event currency for Chocolate Meowstic and other customs over seven days ([PokéFarm Q Wiki](https://pokefarm.wiki/Watery_Woes)). The fourth is a **rate boost that debuts permanent art**. PokéRogue's Halloween 2024 doubled wild shiny rates and introduced new shiny variants "planned to be definitive" ([PokéRogue Wiki](https://wiki.pokerogue.net/events:halloween2024)). That avoids FOMO altogether, because the event is a debut rather than the only chance.

Windows range from one day to one month and cluster around one to two weeks.

| Game | Example | Window | Tradeable | Counts in dex |

The dex column matters most for Dexora. PokéFarm's wiki states the split plainly: Variants "have a Pokédex entry and hatch from an Egg", while Custom Sprites "do not count towards the Pokédex as unique entries" ([PokéFarm Q Wiki](https://pokefarm.wiki/Exclusive_Pok%C3%A9mon)). When a limited-time entry counts towards completion, every player who misses it is left with a permanent gap. When it is a look, a completionist loses nothing. PokéFarm also does not let its customs stack with colour tiers: they "can never be Shiny, Albino, or Melanistic" ([PokéFarm Q Wiki](https://pokefarm.wiki/Custom_Sprite)). Dexora does not need that rule. Most of its tiers are filters, and those apply to any base art.

## Resentment centres on evolution locks, missed announcements and overuse

Pokémon GO is the warning. Its first costume, Santa Hat Pikachu (December 2016), evolved into a Raichu that kept the hat ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/insertcoin/2016/12/22/heres-what-happens-when-you-evolve-santa-pikachu-in-pokemon-go/)). Later costumes were often locked from evolving. By Fashion Week 2023, players asked "why they would make an event costume Pokemon and not allow it to evolve" and "At least give me the opportunity to remove the stupid hat" ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/pokemon/pokemon-go-players-slam-pointless-customed-pokemon-2393411/)). A 2022 community thread said costumes "used to be fun at the beginning" but had become "a nuisance" ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/pokemon/news-anyone-else-care-costume-pokemon-the-pokemon-go-community-discusses-efficacy-dressed-up-monsters)). One blogger called the evolve rules "all wildly convoluted": Butterfree has a bow that Caterpie and Metapod lack ([spuf.org](https://spuf.org/2022/11/04/another-ramble-about-costumed-pokemon/)). The pattern is consistent. A costume that is rare and still evolves is loved. A frequent costume that ends its line is clutter.

PRO's complaints are about scheduling, not art. In a 2025 suggestion thread a player called the gaps between events "dead time", said they had missed a Bloodmoon Ursaluna because Discord never clearly announced it, and asked for a fixed annual calendar. Another player wanted events capped at a month and an icon showing what can be hunted ([PRO forum](https://pokemonrevolution.net/forum/topic/272897-annual-routine-of-events/)). That thread had only four posters, so treat it as anecdote, but every complaint in it is about information rather than content. Vortex shows the positive side. Christmas Caterpie returned in 2020 with its evolutions, Rotom (Halloween) was re-released two years after it first appeared, and Star Wars Day on 4 May became a yearly series ([Vortex Wiki](https://wiki.pokemon-vortex.com/wiki/Events)). Vortex also shows what not to do: its 2015 Christmas Pikachu required owning an earlier event Pikachu, so missing one event also cost you the next.

DelugeRPG, the game Dexora is modelled on, makes event Pokémon untradeable and gives them "about 40% more exp" ([DelugeRPG Forums](https://www.delugerpg.net/viewtopic.php?t=30539)). The EXP bonus breaks Dexora's "a look, never a strength" rule. The trade ban keeps "you were there" meaningful, but it also means a missed event can never be made up. I found no evidence that DelugeRPG draws custom holiday art. Its credited sprites come from Smogon projects and one named contributor ([DelugeRPG Updates](https://www.delugerpg.com/updates)), so its "event Pokémon" may be ordinary species with a flag. That point is unverified, because the site returned 403.

## Ready-made art has run out, so plan to edit sprites

No ready-made pool fits. PokeAPI's official event forms are the cosplay Pikachu (10080–10085), the cap Pikachu (10094–10099, 10148, 10158, 10160) and Spiky-eared Pichu, and `forms.js` already ships them ([PokeAPI/sprites](https://github.com/PokeAPI/sprites)). The largest real costume set is PokeMiners' Pokémon GO dump: **218 costume icons across 108 species**, with Pikachu alone at 34. They are 256px shaded 3D renders, and the repo says "This repo is for educational use only" and "All content found within this repo is the property of The Pokemon Company and Niantic" ([PokeMiners/pogo_assets](https://github.com/PokeMiners/pogo_assets)). They would clash beside FireRed and Black/White pixel art and carry the most explicit warning of any source. The useful part is the event codes (`cHOLIDAY_2016`, `cHALLOWEEN_2017`, `cFALL_2023_NOEVOLVE`), which serve as a catalogue of which costumes The Pokémon Company has already approved, as ideas for a brief. Smogon's Create-A-Pokémon designs are new species with no published reuse terms ([Smogon CAP](https://www.smogon.com/cap/)), so they do not fit "variants of existing species".

Community art is free on the usual terms. The Smogon Sprite Project's sprites "are also free for use as long as the spriter or the project itself is credited" and "free for non-profit use" ([Smogon](https://www.smogon.com/forums/threads/smogon-sprite-project.3647722/)). PokéCommunity packs mostly say "free to use and edit with credits" ([PokéCommunity](https://www.pokecommunity.com/threads/earthretha-fakemon-pack.527306/)). However, no pack of holiday or costume Pokémon in BW or HGSS style turned up on PokéCommunity, itch.io or DeviantArt. The finds were one-off 50px icons such as Ultragriffy's Christmas Plusle, offered "to use as display pictures or pokemoticons" ([DeviantArt](https://www.deviantart.com/ultragriffy/art/Christmas-Minun-419733037)).

Commissions are cheap but cover only a few sprites. Listed prices run **$12 for a 50×50 sprite and $30 for a 128×128 BW-style sprite** ([Tigerhorse](https://tigerhorse-pixel-commissions.carrd.co)). Fiverr gigs run about $20 an hour, or up to about $50 for a small set ([Fiverr: pollorenz](https://fiverr.com/pollorenz/make-sprite-of-custom-pokemon); [Fiverr: lightsabit](https://www.fiverr.com/lightsabit)). Some sellers charge extra for a commercial licence ([Fiverr: dogmatical](https://fiverr.com/dogmatical/do-pixel-art-or-sheet-for-video-game)). So a three-sprite line costs very roughly $40–$150 (my estimate). If you commission, get written permission for use in a free browser game and for derived tier filters and recolours, and record how the artist wants to be credited.

The legal position is the same for every source. PokeAPI's CC0 explicitly does not cover the images, which remain The Pokémon Company's ([PokeAPI LICENCE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PokeAPI/sprites/master/LICENCE.txt)). PixelLab's terms let you own your output but put "full legal responsibility" for third-party rights on you ([PixelLab ToS](https://www.pixellab.ai/termsofservice)). Nintendo has taken down Pokémon Uranium, Pokémon Prism, 562 Game Jolt games and, in March 2024, the Relic Castle fan-game repository ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Prism); [GGRecon](https://ggrecon.com/articles/hundreds-of-fan-made-pokemon-games-have-reportedly-been-culled)). The lowest-risk event art is a custom edit of an existing species: credited, non-commercial, never sold or used for promotion. That is the position Dexora already holds.

## Edit the real sprite; use AI only for the accessory

The recolour standard closest to official is Smogon CAP's shiny rule. Shinies are "palette swaps of the winning sprite entry. No other modifications", and every new colour must keep "the same relative hue and brightness" as the colour it replaces ([Smogon](https://www.smogon.com/forums/posts/10645412)). That is how the games' own shinies were made ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Palette_Swap)). In Aseprite's indexed mode it is a palette edit: change one entry and every pixel using it changes ([Aseprite docs](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/color-mode/)). In Pillow it is a hand-written colour-to-colour map per species. Use a map, not a global hue rotation, because a rotation also moves the outline and eye colours, and that is what makes a recolour look fake.

An accessory changes the outline, and a changed outline is what makes a Pokémon read as "event" at Box-row size. Draw the accessory once at native scale using the game's outline colour. Give each species a hand-entered `(x, y, flip)` anchor and composite the accessory at integer offsets with no resampling. Then re-outline the result so the hat and body share one 1px edge. I found no public source on how GO or fan engines place hats, so anchors entered by hand are the method, and at three sprites per event that is minutes of work. Match each species' own generation style: a BW-shaded hat on a FireRed sprite will look pasted on.

General image models produce what the `unfake` author calls fake pixel art: "There's no concept of hard edges, clean pixels, or fixed palettes" ([DEV](https://dev.to/jenissimo/how-to-tame-your-ai-pixel-art-3pk5)). The fix is a pipeline: detect the pixel scale, align the grid, quantise with Wu's algorithm to a fixed palette, downscale each block to its dominant colour, and clean up stray pixels. The MIT-licensed `unfake` package (pip, Python 3.10+) does all of this, including quantising to a palette file and flood-filling the background to transparent ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/unfake/)). Two pixel-specific services suit the job. Retro Diffusion's RD Plus accepts an uploaded palette and outputs transparency, at roughly $0.015–$0.18 per image ([Scenario](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/retro-diffusion-models-the-essentials/); [listing](https://mcpservers.org/fr/servers/retro-diffusion/retro-diffusion-mcp)). PixelLab offers "True inpainting" from a reference image ([PixelLab](https://www.pixellab.ai/)). Inpainting a masked region on the real sprite is the only AI route that keeps a named species on-model. Generating a whole Pokémon from text will drift. No independent side-by-side test of these tools exists, so try both on a single sprite before committing.

A build script can check the quality bar so that a sprite that does not look official fails the build. The checks: same canvas as the source; alpha only 0 or 255; unique opaque colours no more than the source's count plus about four for the accessory, and no more than 15–16 on a GBA source; every pixel touching transparency uses an outline colour the source uses; and nothing outside the source's mask except the accessory region. Smogon's sprite rules ("no more than 15 unique colors", one shared palette across front and back) are the published reference for those numbers ([Smogon](https://www.smogon.com/forums/posts/9043514/)).

## Recommended first event: Witch's Hat Gastly, Halloween 2026

**What.** The Gastly line (Gastly, Haunter, Gengar) wearing a witch's hat. This is my recommendation, drawn from the evidence rather than taken from any source. A whole line of three FireRed sprites means the costume carries through evolution, which avoids GO's most-cited complaint. A Ghost line fits Halloween without adding to Pikachu's existing costumes. A floating gas ball takes a hat easily. One accessory drawn once covers all three sprites. Keep it to one line. PRO's 96 forms belong to a game with years of events behind it, while PokéFarm and DelugeRPG give one or a few per event, and Dexora's costume novelty is still unspent.

**Window.** Fourteen days of real dates (`dayKey`), for example 27 October–9 November. That sits at the common one-to-two-week length, under the one-month cap PRO's players suggested, and overlaps GO's Halloween (27 October–5 November) ([Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemongo/events.shtml)). Publish the News entry, with both dates, a week before the event opens. Show the event in `engine.events()` as a gold-rimmed card with a countdown ring, and put an Events-board card naming the map and the species. That answers PRO's "I wasn't told" and "show what can be hunted".

**Storage that keeps saves safe.** Make each costumed Pokémon a look: `form: "look"`, in `LOOK_FAMILIES` with Gastly, Haunter and Gengar, appended at the end of the forms like every look since Unown. The three species entries ship in the build before the window opens and stay permanently, because a shipped form is never evicted. The calendar decides spawns only, never the save shape. That keeps "append, never insert" intact, gives a held Pokémon a valid index after the event ends, and leaves `padDex`, trading and tier rows unchanged. Add one data field (for example `event: "halloween"`) and one rule: `rollLook` excludes an event look outside its window. Dexora's existing alternative entries (Spiky-eared Pichu, the costumes) are already species, so nothing new is needed in the save. Before shipping, check two things in the code. First, `owns` should treat the family as held by any one drawing, so a missed hat never blocks a generation medal. Second, the Pokédex rank and Pokédex binder count `SPECIES.length` shares; event looks must be left out of those, or the top rank becomes impossible for anyone who missed a year. That second point is the PokéFarm Custom Sprite lesson.

**Delivery.** Use both of the models players reward. The guaranteed claim works like DelugeRPG's, without the EXP perk: an Events-board task during the window (for example, catch ten Ghost types) pays one Witch's Hat Gastly, saved before any animation plays, like a check-in. The chase works like PRO's: on the Pokémon Tower (`tower`), any Gastly-line encounter during the window rolls the hat at a set share. Tier rolls are unchanged, so a Witch's Hat Shiny Gengar exists and a filter tier draws over the hat. Strip tiers lock themselves because no strip exists (`hasShowdown` reads the files). Tune the share so that someone who hunts the Tower for a sitting meets one. That is a design target for check.mjs to measure, not a sourced rate. No perk, no League bonus and no alpha rule change: it is a look, never a strength.

**Evolution, trade and return.** It evolves with the costume kept, Gastly to Haunter to Gengar, through `fetch-evolutions`' look rows (as Blue Flabébé becomes Blue Floette). Trading is allowed. PRO and older PokéFarm customs show that event Pokémon keep their meaning as tradeable goods, and trading is the catch-up route for a player who missed the window. `check_save_pace` already keeps forged saves out of trading. It returns every Halloween: the same hat, plus at most one new line a year. That makes it a yearly ritual like Vortex's 4 May, and never a prerequisite for anything else.

## Art pipeline and prompts for the owner

The owner makes one asset, the hat. The developer does the rest by script. Proposed file layout: `art/events/halloween/witch-hat.png` (the owner's original, any size), `tools/build_event.py` (cleans it with `unfake` to a fixed palette, composites it at hand-entered anchors from `art/events/halloween/anchors.json` onto the three FireRed sprites and their shiny versions, re-outlines, runs the quality checks above, and writes `public/sprites/` plus the shiny folder), and an `npm run event` entry in CLAUDE.md's generated-files table. If the hat-only composite looks stiff on Gengar, fall back to inpainting: the owner uploads the real sprite, upscaled ×4 nearest-neighbour, masks the area above the head, and generates the hat in place. The same script then cleans that output onto the sprite's palette plus four colours. Credit any outside spriter on the credits page in the same way as SageDeoxys.

Prompt for the standalone accessory (preferred: one hat reused three times):

```
A pointed witch's hat, pixel art game sprite asset, single object on a plain
flat magenta (#FF00FF) background, viewed from the front-left at a slight 3/4
angle as if worn by a creature facing left. Exactly 24 pixels wide, hard
1-pixel dark outline in #101010, flat cel shading with 3 tones of deep purple
and one orange band around the brim, no anti-aliasing, no gradients, no
dithering, no soft shadows, no glow, maximum 6 colours. Gen 3 Game Boy Advance
Pokemon sprite style. No creature, no text, no frame.
```

Prompt for inpainting onto the real sprite (masked region above the head only):

```
Add a pointed purple witch's hat with an orange band, sitting tilted on the
top of the head, drawn in exactly the same pixel art style as the rest of the
sprite: same pixel size, same 1-pixel dark outline colour, same flat shading
direction (light from top-left), no new anti-aliasing, no gradients, colours
taken from the existing palette plus at most 4 new ones. Do not change any
pixel outside the masked area. Transparent background.
```

Generation tips for the owner. Use a flat magenta or transparent background, never white or a gradient, so the cleanup can find the edge. Ask for the native pixel size, but expect the cleanup script to do the real resizing. Generate four to eight candidates and pick by silhouette, not by detail, because detail is lost at 64px. Never name the Pokémon in a text-to-image prompt for a whole sprite: it drifts off-model, and some services may refuse trademarked names (unverified). A recolour event needs no prompt at all. The owner picks a target ramp, for example "pumpkin orange, 4 tones, light to dark", and the script maps the species' own ramps onto it under Smogon's same-relative-brightness rule.

Pitfalls, in the order they would hurt. Inserting rather than appending the look would shift every later save position. Counting event looks towards dex rank or completion turns a missed year into a permanent gap. Drawing only the base stage makes evolving cost the costume, which is GO's complaint. Shipping GO icons or bilinear-upscaled AI output puts semi-transparent edges and a second art style beside the FireRed sprites; ship native pixels and let CSS scale them. Mixing generation styles on one line looks pasted on. Making one event's look a requirement for a later event repeats Vortex's Pikachu. Running events with no News entry, no dates and no hunt icon repeats PRO's grievance. Giving the look a stat or EXP perk makes a limited-time item a strength that latecomers can never get.

## Conclusion

The research turns the event question into a storage question. Every grievance players voice about limited-time Pokémon comes from one of three choices: the costume lives in the completion count, the costume is a dead end in its line, or the event is invisible until it is over. Dexora's look mechanism already solves the first two by construction. A look is an appended species that the family's single slot rolls, that carries through evolution, and that a family can hold by any one drawing. The third is solved by tools Dexora already has (News, the HUD event card, the Events board). So the first event needs about one new rule (the window gate on `rollLook`) and one script, not a new system.

The real limit is art supply. Official event art in Dexora's style is used up, and every other source is either a different style or someone else's labour. An event series is therefore sustainable only at a pace of about one accessory a year that the owner can make and a script can check. Holding to that pace also keeps costumes rare enough to stay special, which is the pace Pokémon GO lost.
